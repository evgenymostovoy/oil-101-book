# Recall profile: narrative and mechanism strong, anchors weak, plausible substitution risk

First study session (Unit 01) showed a clear pattern: causal chains and
market mechanisms come back well unprompted (Rockefeller's refining
monopoly, TRC→OPEC handoff, Saudi 1985–86 quota collapse, the
demand-destruction mechanism), but anchors — dates, prices, names —
are weak or distorted (Drake's well placed on the wrong coast, Persia
1908 and Saudi 1938 merged into "1910–1920", "netback pricing"
irretrievable). Most notably, gaps get filled with *plausible
substitutions from adjacent professional knowledge*: "Platts and PRAs"
offered in place of formula pricing, "OPEC refused dollars in 1973"
invented from the petrodollar meme.

## Implications

- Quiz anchors (dates, numbers, names) explicitly and grade them
  strictly — narrative fluency will otherwise mask missing anchors.
- Treat confident-sounding specifics with extra scrutiny: this learner's
  wrong answers are well-formed, not hesitant.
- A compact price-timeline reference doc (1859–2026 anchor events) is a
  candidate artifact if 01-cycle-timeline keeps failing on review.
